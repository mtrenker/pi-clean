# Pattern catalog

Adapted from Lauren Tan's MIT-licensed `unslop` skill in pstack. See
[../NOTICE.md](../NOTICE.md) for provenance and the upstream license.

Read this only for a dedicated cleanup pass or when the main skill leaves a call unclear.
Its guardrails still apply.

## Content

- **Puffery:** Cut "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving landscape", "setting the
  stage for", "indelible mark", and similar claims. State what happened.
- **Name-dropping:** Do not list outlets, companies, or tools without context. Pick the
  relevant one and state what it did or said.
- **Empty participles:** Delete "highlighting the need", "ensuring reliability",
  "showcasing", and "fostering", or replace them with a fact.
- **Promotion:** Replace "vibrant", "groundbreaking", "renowned", "stunning", and
  "must-have" with a neutral description.
- **Vague attribution:** Name the source, quote concrete output, or remove the claim.
- **Formulaic challenge framing:** Replace "Despite challenges, X continues to thrive"
  with the specific constraint and result.

## Language

- Prefer plain words over additionally, crucial, delve, enhance, foster, garner, interplay,
  intricate, landscape, pivotal, robust, seamless, showcase, tapestry, testament,
  underscore, and vibrant.
- Replace "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", and "features" with is or has when exact.
- State the point instead of writing "not just X, but Y".
- Use the natural number of examples. Do not force three.
- Use one consistent technical term instead of rotating synonyms.
- Use "from X to Y" only for endpoints on a real scale.

## Style

- Avoid em dashes. End the sentence or use a comma. Parentheses can hold short clarifiers
  or citations. En dashes can mark numeric ranges.
- Use colons for lists and examples, not as a substitute for rewriting two clauses.
- Use bold to name an item, not to emphasize every proper noun or acronym.
- Convert a bold label plus colon that restates its line to prose. Keep a useful bold
  lead-in when it names an item and the following text adds new detail.
- Use sentence case headings, straight quotes, and no decorative emoji in new prose.
- These rules target decoration, never structure. Keep headings, lists, tables,
  descriptive link text, and text labels beside glyphs.
- Never rewrite typography inside code, quoted source text, or license text.

## Communication artifacts

- Remove chatbot phrases, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, sycophancy, and narration such as
  "I've tightened this up". Answer or provide the edited text.

## Filler

- "In order to" becomes "to". "Due to the fact that" becomes "because". Delete "it is
  important to note that".
- Reduce stacked hedges to one accurate qualifier. Keep genuine uncertainty.
- Delete generic conclusions such as "The future looks bright" unless a concrete next step
  replaces them.

## Jargon

Replace decorative metaphor nouns with the mechanism's real name or a plain term. Common
examples include substrate, wedge, vector, locus, nexus, modality, paradigm, bedrock,
endgame, north star, flywheel, gold-plating, and ratchet. A term is fine when it is the
project's actual name for the thing.

## Plain speech

- State what something does, not how it feels. Name the behavior or measurement.
- Split a sentence when a reader must backtrack. Keep one main idea per sentence.
- Prefer active voice when the actor matters. Passive voice is fine when the actor is
  unknown or irrelevant.
- Replace weak adverbs with a stronger verb or measured result.
- Prefer use, help, many, and if over utilize, facilitate, numerous, and "in the event that".
